Nature & Scope of Advice

Mutual Solutions provides personalised financial advice in the areas below. We recommend products from the providers available to us, and we will tell you if we ever recommend a product from outside that list and why.

What we advise on

  • Personal risk insurance – life, trauma, total and permanent disability, income protection, mortgage repayment protection, health and medical insurance, and related risk insurance.
  • Business insurance – key person, shareholder and buy-sell protection, business overheads and business continuity cover, where applicable.
  • KiwiSaver – provider selection, fund choice and contribution strategy.
  • Selected managed funds and investment products – limited to managed funds and portfolio investment entities offered by providers we hold agency or distribution arrangements with.
  • Residential home loans – first home, refinancing, construction and top-ups, under our separate Home Loan Disclosure Guide.
  • Ongoing servicing of any product arranged through us, including annual reviews and claims support.

What we do not advise on

  • Legal advice
  • Tax advice
  • Estate planning advice
  • General insurance, such as house, contents, vehicle or travel insurance
  • Direct shares, bonds, derivatives, foreign exchange, cryptocurrency, discretionary investment management or foreign pension transfers
  • Personal loans, business lending, equipment finance or other non-residential lending, unless expressly agreed with you in writing

Where something is outside our scope, we will tell you and, where we can, refer you to a suitable specialist. We do not execute investment transactions on your behalf.

Information on this website is general in nature. It becomes personalised advice only once we have agreed a written scope of service with you and you have received a Statement of Advice.

Fees, Expenses & Commissions

For standard advice, you usually do not pay us a fee. Instead, the product provider pays Mutual Solutions a commission when a policy, loan, KiwiSaver membership or investment is put in place through us. Commission rates vary by provider and product, and we will show you the specific commission that applies to any recommendation in your Statement of Advice.

For a plain-English explanation of how this works, see How commissions work.

Commission we may receive

  • Insurance providers may pay an upfront commission when cover starts, and an ongoing (renewal) commission each year the policy stays in force.
  • KiwiSaver and investment providers may pay a commission when you join, and an ongoing commission based on your balance.
  • Lenders may pay a commission when a home loan settles, and in some cases an ongoing trail commission.

Fees you may pay

Any fee payable by you must be agreed with you in writing before you become liable for it. Client-paid fees may apply in these situations:

  • Products that pay no commission – quoted case by case and agreed in writing.
  • Stand-alone complex consultation – $250 + GST per hour, only where agreed with you in writing beforehand.
  • Commission clawback – see the next section.

Commission Clawback & Recoupment

When an insurance policy is cancelled or materially reduced within the first 24 months, the insurer can require Mutual Solutions to repay some or all of the upfront commission it received. This is called a clawback.

Recoupment fee

What you may be invoiced

If you cancel or materially reduce an insurance policy within 24 months of its start date and the insurer claws back commission, Mutual Solutions may invoice you the lesser of:

(a) the actual commission amount clawed back by the provider; or

(b) $3,000 + GST.

For example, if $1,500 of commission is clawed back after 15 months, you may be invoiced $1,500 + GST, payable on the 20th of the following month.

This fee applies only where it has been properly disclosed to you and agreed in writing before your cover starts. No recoupment fee applies once a policy has been in force for longer than the 24-month clawback period, and none applies where the insurer does not claw back commission.

Conflicts of Interest

Because product providers pay us commission, and because we work with a selected group of providers, conflicts of interest can arise. Providers may also offer training, hospitality, technology support or other small benefits from time to time. Any benefit that could reasonably influence our advice will be disclosed to you in writing.

How we manage this

  1. Every recommendation is based on your needs, goals and circumstances, assessed through a written needs analysis before any remuneration is considered.
  2. We compare the options available from our providers where appropriate, using a product research matrix.
  3. Commissions, fees and any soft-dollar benefits are disclosed in your Statement of Advice.
  4. We keep records of our advice and the reasons for it.
  5. Our advice is reviewed internally, and independently where required, and the adviser completes an annual conflicts attestation and ongoing training.
  6. If you are unhappy with how a conflict has been handled, you can raise a complaint with us and escalate it to FSCL at no cost.

Where we collect it from

  • Directly from you, in meetings, forms, email and phone calls
  • Third parties you authorise, such as your employer, accountant, doctor or a credit reporting agency
  • Insurers, lenders, KiwiSaver and investment providers, in relation to products you hold or apply for
  • Public sources, such as the Companies Office, LINZ and the Financial Service Providers Register
  • Service providers that help us verify identity or process applications

How we use it

  • To provide the advice and services you ask for
  • To obtain quotes and submit applications to product providers
  • To service your products, including claims, top-ups, switches and refinancing
  • To carry out reviews of your cover and circumstances
  • To meet our legal and regulatory duties, including under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the Code of Professional Conduct for Financial Advice Services
  • To meet our obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2009
  • To communicate with you, including product and service updates, which you can opt out of at any time
  • To keep the records we are required to keep

Who we share it with

  • Insurers, lenders, KiwiSaver and investment providers, for quotes, underwriting, applications, issuance and servicing
  • Compliance reviewers and auditors who check our advice files
  • Providers of our CRM, document storage, email and cloud services, and the form service that delivers website enquiries to us
  • AML/CFT identity verification providers
  • Regulators and government agencies where the law requires it, including the Financial Markets Authority, Inland Revenue and the Department of Internal Affairs

We never sell personal information. Third parties who receive your information may use it in line with their own privacy policies, for example an insurer's medical assessors in the event of a claim.

Cloud and overseas storage

Some of your information is stored or processed using secure cloud services such as Google Drive, Microsoft 365, Dropbox, our CRM, our website enquiry form service and similar systems. Some of these services store data outside New Zealand. Where that happens, we take reasonable steps to make sure appropriate protections apply, including encryption in transit and at rest, multi-factor authentication, and Privacy Act 2020 safeguards in our supplier agreements.

How long we keep it

We keep client records for at least seven years after our relationship ends, as required by law and regulation, and then destroy them securely.

Our Duties to You

Under the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, Mutual Solutions Limited and anyone giving advice on its behalf must:

  • Give priority to your interests by taking all reasonable steps to make sure our advice is not materially influenced by our own interests
  • Exercise care, diligence and skill in providing advice
  • Meet the standards of competence, knowledge and skill set by the Code of Professional Conduct for Financial Advice Services
  • Meet the standards of ethical behaviour, conduct and client care set by the Code

More information about these duties is available from the Financial Markets Authority at fma.govt.nz.
