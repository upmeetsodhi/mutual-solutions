Self-Employed Mortgage Advice NZ: Getting Approved for a Home Loan

Published: June 18, 2026 | Reviewed: October 21, 2026 | Read time: 8 minutes

By: Upmeet Sodhi, Financial Adviser (FSP 250405)

The Self-Employed Mortgage Challenge

If you're self-employed or run your own business, you already know getting a mortgage is different. Banks treat self-employed applicants more cautiously because your income can fluctuate. But getting approved for a mortgage as a self-employed person is absolutely possible. You just need to understand what lenders are looking for and present your application strategically.

What Lenders Want From Self-Employed Applicants

1. Proof of income

Requirements vary by lender and application. Expect to provide current evidence that helps a lender understand both personal income and the business behind it. Commonly requested items include:

  • Recent accountant-prepared financial statements
  • Relevant income-tax returns or summaries
  • Current management accounts, where they help explain recent trading
  • Personal and business bank statements requested by the lender

The lender will assess whether the evidence supports sustainable income and affordable repayments. A change in income is not automatically good or bad, but the lender may ask for context and more recent information.

2. Business Stability

Lenders want to see your business is stable and likely to continue. They assess this by looking at:

  • How long you have been self-employed
  • Whether you have regular, recurring clients
  • Industry trends (is your sector growing or declining?)
  • Your business plan and future prospects

3. Strong Deposit

A 20% deposit is a useful benchmark for an owner-occupier application, but it is not a universal approval requirement. Reserve Bank LVR rules limit the share of new bank lending above 80% LVR rather than banning lower-deposit loans, and lenders still apply their own criteria:

  • 20% deposit means the loan is not classed as high-LVR under the current owner-occupier settings
  • Less than 20% may still be possible, depending on the lender, its available high-LVR capacity, your circumstances, and any applicable exemption or scheme
  • A larger deposit reduces the LVR, but it does not replace the lender's income, affordability, credit, and policy checks

4. Good Credit Score & Debt-to-Income Ratio

Lenders check:

  • Your credit history (a clean record with no defaults, missed payments, or excessive credit applications helps significantly)
  • Existing debts (credit cards, car loans, student loans)
  • Your debt-to-income ratio. Reserve Bank settings restrict the share of new bank lending above DTI 6 for owner-occupiers and DTI 7 for investors; they are speed limits on bank lending, not automatic approval caps for every borrower
Practical point: Existing debt affects affordability. Before repaying or restructuring anything, check how the lender is likely to assess it and whether using cash that way would reduce your deposit or emergency reserve.

Self-Employed Mortgage Application Strategy

Step 1: Get Your Finances in Order

Before approaching lenders, ensure your financial documents are clean and organized:

  • File your tax returns on time every year
  • Keep detailed financial records
  • Separate business and personal finances completely
  • Pay yourself a consistent salary (shows regular income)
  • Avoid large unexplained transactions

Step 2: Gather Documentation Early

Don't wait until you're ready to buy. Start gathering these documents months in advance:

  • Recent tax returns or summaries relevant to your business structure
  • Accountant-prepared business financial statements
  • Personal and business bank statements for the period the lender requests
  • Personal financial statement
  • Business plan (if you're less than 3 years established)

Step 3: Choose the Right Lender

Not all lenders are created equal when it comes to self-employed mortgages:

  • Banks: Criteria, document requirements, pricing, and appetite differ between lenders
  • Non-bank lenders: May assess some situations differently; rates, fees, and terms need to be compared carefully
  • Mortgage advisers: Can explain the lenders available to them and help present verified information consistently

Step 4: Tell Your Story

Lenders want to understand your business. Provide context for your financials:

  • How long have you been self-employed?
  • What's your business growth trajectory?
  • Do you have long-term contracts or regular clients?
  • What's the market outlook for your industry?

Common Reasons Self-Employed Mortgage Applications Get Declined

  • Limited trading history: The lender may require more evidence or may not accept the application under its standard policy.
  • Income that has changed: The lender may ask what caused the change and what current evidence supports the income used.
  • Inconsistent documentation: Tax returns don't match bank statements or financials. Clean this up.
  • High existing debt: This may reduce borrowing capacity. Check the likely effect before deciding whether to repay debt or retain funds for the deposit and other costs.
  • Deposit or LVR outside the lender's appetite: Options depend on the lender, borrower type, property, and any applicable exemption or scheme.
  • Adverse credit history: Check the facts and discuss the lender's criteria before making repeated applications.

Income Types: How Lenders View Different Self-Employment

Stable Income (Easier to Borrow)

  • Contracts with long-term clients (3+ year agreements)
  • Recurring revenue (subscriptions, retainers)
  • Professional services (law, accounting, engineering)

Variable Income (Harder to Borrow)

  • Project-based work without fixed contracts
  • Commission-based income
  • Seasonal businesses
  • Gig economy work

If income is variable, the lender may ask for a longer history, current evidence, or an explanation of how contracts and recurring work support the income used. The exact calculation is lender-specific.

Getting Expert Advice

Self-employed mortgages are more complex than traditional employee mortgages. Working with a mortgage broker or financial adviser who specializes in self-employed lending can:

  • Help you prepare documents in the way lenders want to see them
  • Explain differences in the criteria of the lenders available to the adviser
  • Help compare rates, fees, features, and conditions
  • Reduce avoidable rework by checking the evidence before an application is lodged
