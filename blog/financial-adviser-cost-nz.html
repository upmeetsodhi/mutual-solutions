Financial Adviser Fees in NZ: What Advice May Cost

Direct fees, provider commissions and the disclosures to read before you proceed

Short answer: there is no single standard price. A financial adviser may charge you a direct fee, receive a commission from a product provider, or use both. Before you proceed, the adviser should explain any applicable fee and disclose relevant commissions or other conflicts. Mutual Solutions offers a free initial consultation; any client-paid fee that may apply is set out and agreed in writing before you become liable.

"How much does a financial adviser cost?" is a sensible question to ask before the first meeting. The answer depends on the service, the adviser and whether a product provider pays commission. Here are the main models and the information to request.

Provider-paid commission

An insurer, lender or KiwiSaver provider may pay an adviser if a client takes up a product. The amount and structure can vary by provider and product. Commission can create a conflict of interest, so the adviser should explain how they are paid and how they manage that conflict.

Commission does not remove the need to check the product's price, terms and suitability. Ask which providers the adviser can consider, whether any options sit outside that scope, and whether a direct fee could still apply.

Client-paid fees

An adviser may charge a direct fee for advice, implementation, ongoing service or another agreed piece of work. Common structures include:

  • a fixed fee for a defined service;
  • an hourly or time-based fee;
  • an ongoing fee, which may be expressed as a dollar amount or a percentage; or
  • a fee that becomes payable in a stated circumstance, such as when agreed commission is reversed.

Hybrid models

Some advisers receive commission and also charge a fee. The important point is not the label; it is whether the adviser clearly explains the service, the amount or basis of any fee, when it becomes payable, relevant commissions, and conflicts that could materially influence the advice.

New Zealand's disclosure rules require specified information about fees, commissions and other incentives to be made available at particular stages of the advice process. You can read the requirements in Schedule 21A of the Financial Markets Conduct Regulations 2014.

What to actually ask before your first meeting

  • Are you paid by commission, fee, or both?
  • If commission, which providers do you receive commission from, and does that limit what you can recommend?
  • If fee-based, what's the total cost for what I need done?
  • Is the first meeting free, and is there any obligation to proceed?

Read the adviser's disclosure information and ask for clarification before agreeing to proceed. A useful answer should identify the service, the scope of providers considered, how the adviser is paid, and any amount or basis of a fee that could become payable.
