KiwiSaver Contribution Rates NZ: 3.5%, 4%, 6%, 8% or 10%?

Reviewed: 21 October 2026 | Read time: 7 minutes

By: Upmeet Sodhi, Financial Adviser (FSP 250405)

Current settings from 1 April 2026: employee contribution options are 3.5%, 4%, 6%, 8% or 10% of before-tax pay. The default minimum employee and compulsory employer rates are generally 3.5%, subject to eligibility and other rules. Eligible members can request a temporary employee-rate reduction to 3% for 3 to 12 months.

What you'll learn

  • How compulsory employer contributions and ESCT work

KiwiSaver contribution options in 2026

One of the most common questions I hear is: "How much should I contribute to KiwiSaver?" There is no rate that suits everyone. The right starting point depends on cash flow, employer arrangements, first-home or retirement timing, other debts and savings, and your fund choice.

1. Employee contribution rates

From 1 April 2026, employees can contribute 3.5%, 4%, 6%, 8% or 10% of before-tax pay. If you do not select a rate, the default is 3.5%. Eligible members who need short-term relief can apply to Inland Revenue for a temporary reduction to 3% for between 3 and 12 months.

  • Use the rate-change form in myIR or give a completed KS2 form to your employer.
  • A higher rate increases current deductions and may increase long-term savings, but reduces take-home pay.
  • A contribution-rate decision is separate from choosing an appropriate KiwiSaver fund and provider.

Check the current rules on Inland Revenue's employee contributions page.

2. Employer contributions

From 1 April 2026, the compulsory employer contribution rate is generally 3.5% for eligible employees who contribute through payroll. Exceptions apply, including some employees under 18 or over 65 and some employment arrangements. Employer superannuation contribution tax (ESCT) is deducted, so the net amount reaching your KiwiSaver account is less than the gross employer contribution.

Check your own arrangement: some employers contribute more than the compulsory minimum, while some total-remuneration agreements treat employer contributions differently. Read your employment agreement and payslip, and ask payroll if anything is unclear.

Inland Revenue explains compulsory contributions and exceptions on its employer contribution page.

3. Voluntary Contributions: When to Consider Them

Beyond your regular employee contribution, you can make voluntary contributions to KiwiSaver. This might make sense if:

  • You have extra income and want to accelerate your retirement savings
  • You want to maximise the annual government contribution (see below)
  • You're self-employed and want to boost your retirement savings
  • You're planning a first-home withdrawal and want to build eligible savings

The annual government contribution

For the KiwiSaver year from 1 July to 30 June, eligible members can receive 25 cents from the government for each dollar they contribute, up to a maximum of $260.72. To receive the maximum, you need to contribute at least $1,042.86 of your own money during the year. From 1 July 2025, people with taxable income over $180,000 are not eligible.

How the Government Top-Up Works

  • The contribution is based on your eligible member contributions during the KiwiSaver year.
  • Eligibility and the maximum can be reduced if you join, turn 18 or reach the withdrawal age part-way through the year.
  • The payment is made directly into your KiwiSaver account after the end of the KiwiSaver year

Example: If you are eligible for the full year and contribute $1,042.86 of your own money, the maximum government contribution under the current settings is $260.72. The payment goes to your KiwiSaver account, not your bank account.

Before topping up: check your eligibility and year-to-date contributions in myIR. Rules and thresholds can change. Inland Revenue's KiwiSaver changes page records the current settings used here.

How to choose a contribution rate

  • Cash flow: compare the deduction at each rate with essential spending, emergency savings and debt repayments.
  • Employer arrangement: confirm the employer rate, ESCT effect and any matching or total-remuneration terms.
  • Government contribution: check eligibility and whether payroll deductions are likely to reach the annual threshold.
  • Time horizon: consider first-home plans, expected retirement timing and when the money may be available.
  • Fund and fees: contribution rate, investment risk, provider service and fees are different decisions, but they affect the same outcome.

Common KiwiSaver Contribution Mistakes

  • Using an old rate: the default employee and compulsory employer rates changed on 1 April 2026.
  • Assuming everyone receives the government contribution: age, residence, income and part-year eligibility rules matter.
  • Ignoring ESCT: the net employer contribution credited to your account is lower than the gross rate.
  • Choosing a rate without reviewing the fund: contributions, risk level and fees all influence the long-term result.

Getting personalised advice

Your appropriate KiwiSaver contribution rate depends on your income, expenses, other debts and savings, first-home plans, retirement goals and tolerance for investment risk. This article is general information, not a personal recommendation.
